A driver cycle covers the whole job: travelling to the restaurant, waiting for the food, delivering, and repositioning for the next order. Two batched orders share one cycle plus a short second drop, so each carries a little over half. Because driver capacity is the limit, batching raises the number of orders one salaried driver can complete, and that is what lowers the cost of each. How many orders can be paired depends on order density and on predicting when each kitchen will finish.

One model drives all three columns. The predicted ready time is learned from captured preparation intervals; the customer ETA adds the handover delay and the travel time for that zone, both also learned from history; batchability is two orders whose predicted ready times fall close enough together for one trip. Ranges run median to 90th percentile, because a delivery promise without a confidence level cannot be planned against. Horizons are stated in real-world minutes and scaled to the simulated clock.
